# Hooks

The hook is the first second and a half. Most viewers decide there, and most of them decide with
the sound off, so a hook has to work as a picture and as words on screen before it works as audio.

Write the hook before the rest of the script. Everything after it is paying off what it promised.

## Where to find them

Mine these before writing, and keep what you find in the campaign folder as `hooks.md` — the
ad, the hook type, and one line on why it works. Ten minutes here beats an hour of rewrites.

| Source | What it's good for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok Creative Center → Top Ads** | Real paid ads, filterable by industry, region and objective. Watch only the first two seconds of twenty of them. | Free, no login for most views |
| **TikTok Creative Center → Trends → Songs** | Sounds rising in a region. Check the commercial-use flag before putting any of them in a paid ad. | Free |
| **TikTok Commercial Music Library** | Sounds that *are* cleared for business accounts and ads. | Free with a business account |
| **Meta Ad Library** | Every active ad on Facebook and Instagram, searchable by advertiser or keyword. Look at competitors and at big spenders in adjacent categories. Shows no performance data, so an ad that has run for months is the only signal. | Free |
| **Google Ads Transparency Center** | The same for YouTube and Google video ads. | Free |
| **Foreplay** | A swipe-file tool: saves ads from the libraries above and tags them by hook, angle and format. Worth it once there is a team writing ads weekly. | Paid |
| **Motion (creative library)** | Analyses *your own* ad account's hook rate by concept. Research on the way in, measurement on the way out. | Paid |
| **The video generator's effect or viral presets** | Visual-hook ideas: camera moves, transitions and effects that are trending. Browse them; browsing is free, running one is not. | Browse free |
| **Your own Ads Manager** | The only source that tells you what works *for this business*. See *Measuring*. | Free |

Copying is not the point. Note the *shape* of a hook — callout, question, contrarian claim — and
write a new one for this business. Ad copy is protected like any other writing, and a lifted
line fails the swap test in `copy-rules.md` anyway.

## Hook types

Each type below is a shape, not a script. Pick one per variant.

### Verbal hooks — the first line

- **Callout.** Name the viewer. "Dentist? Accountant? Hairdresser?" works because each person who
  hears their own job stops. Name jobs, suburbs or situations, never personal attributes: see the
  personal-attributes rule in `copy-rules.md` — "Struggling with debt?" is a rejection.
- **Specific number.** "Fifteen invoices retyped every Friday." A real, checkable number beats an
  adjective. It has to be true.
- **Contrarian.** "Don't buy an AI tool yet." Works when the rest of the ad earns it.
- **Question the viewer can answer in their head.** "Who still types quotes into Xero by hand?"
- **Promise of a payoff.** "By Saturday lunch this runs itself." It has to pay off inside the ad, or
  on the page the ad links to.
- **Admission.** "We'll tell you if this won't work for you." Rare in ads, so it stands out.

### Visual hooks — the first frame

- **Motion in frame one.** Never open on a static title. The first frame should already be moving —
  a push-in, a character mid-gesture, a hand entering.
- **Pattern interrupt.** Something unexpected for the feed: an object at the wrong scale, a flat
  cartoon in a feed of phone video, a hard colour change.
- **The problem, shown.** A desk buried in paper, an inbox counter climbing. Must be staged or
  illustrated — never presented as a real customer's situation unless it is one.
- **Text-first frame.** The hook line on screen in the first frame, large, inside the safe zone. For
  sound-off viewers this *is* the hook.
- **Before/after split.** Only with real before and after. A generated "after" is a fabricated result.

### Stitch and reply hooks — borrowing a first second

- **Stitch / duet.** Open on a few seconds of someone else's clip, then respond. It is an organic
  TikTok format: the original creator must allow stitching, and putting it in a *paid* ad needs the
  creator's authorisation (TikTok's Spark Ads code, or written permission). Without that, don't.
- **Reply-to-comment.** Open on a real comment from your own post ("does this work for a salon?")
  and answer it. The safe way to get the stitch energy in a paid ad. Get the commenter's permission
  before an ad puts their name and words in front of strangers.
- **Green-screen.** The presenter or character in front of a real page, a real screenshot or a real
  message. The thing behind them must be genuine.

### Sound hooks — the first beat

- **A trending sound**, if and only if it is cleared for commercial use. Most chart songs are not.
- **A hard sound on frame one**: a whoosh, a pop, a tap. Cheap, cleared stock SFX work.
- **The voice starting on word one.** No breath, no intro music bar. The VO script should open on its
  strongest word.

## Rules that apply to every type

- **Readable in 1.5 seconds with the sound off.** If the hook needs audio to make sense, add the words
  on screen.
- **Inside the safe zone.** The top and bottom 250px of a 9:16 are covered by platform UI.
- **The hook is a claim like any other.** Everything in `copy-rules.md` applies — no invented numbers,
  no testimonials, no implied results.
- **One hook per variant.** When testing, change the hook and nothing else, so the test measures it.

## Measuring

Once an ad has spent, the two numbers that judge a hook:

- **Hook rate** — 3-second video plays ÷ impressions. Did the first second stop the scroll?
- **Hold rate** — ThruPlays (or 15-second plays) ÷ 3-second plays. Did the rest keep them?

A high hook rate with a low hold rate means the hook promised something the ad didn't deliver.
Record what each variant scored in the campaign's `hooks.md`, so the next campaign starts from this
business's own evidence rather than someone else's swipe file.
